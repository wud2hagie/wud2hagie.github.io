# Designing a Blended Learning Course on Open edX

**Published:** 2024-09-22 · **Reading time:** 7 min

Building a course on **Open edX** that students actually finish is harder
than it looks. After two years of teaching faculty at Debre Tabor University
how to author digital modules, here are the patterns I keep coming back to.

## 1. Start with the assessment, not the content

Inverted as it sounds: design the *evidence of learning* first. If you can
articulate what a student must demonstrate at the end of week three, the
content needed to get them there becomes obvious.

## 2. Chunk aggressively

A 50-minute lecture recording is, statistically, watched for about 6 minutes.
Break content into 4-to-7 minute segments, each with a single clear learning
objective. Open edX's vertical structure makes this natural — embrace it.

## 3. Use interactive problems as anchors

Each video should be followed by an interactive problem — multiple choice,
drag-and-drop, or a numerical input. Even a single, well-crafted problem
improves retention dramatically.

## 4. Respect the load

Students juggling multiple courses can sustain about 4–6 hours of online
work per week, per course. If your module exceeds that, you will lose them —
no matter how engaging the videos are.

## 5. Iterate based on analytics

Open edX's insights dashboard shows you exactly where students drop off.
Treat this as your "clinic" — review it weekly, and revise the problem
sequences or video segments accordingly.
